# 2026-09-09 — Dream OCR: four engines, and the thing that actually worked

Owner: _"lets combine surya, paddleocr with ppstructure, doctr, tesseract and
build our own dream ocr, with best figurine notation for chess, and our dream ocr
should be trained to be near 100%"._

## What was built

`dream_ocr.py` — four OCR engines behind one interface, aligned by consensus,
then a **chess constraint pass** that replays the move text as a game and repairs
what cannot be legal. Full write-up in `knowledge/20-dream-ocr.md`.

## The honest headline

The four engines are the small half. Chess text is a rigid grammar in which every
move token must be a legal move in a position we already know from the diagram.
Tesseract cannot tell `Nf3` from `Nf8`; legality can prove it. That constraint is
worth more than a fourth engine.

Near-100% is real for MOVE text and false for prose, so confidence is per token
and `verified` marks only what was proved.

## Measured

| | OCR as-is | Dream OCR | Proved certain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Realistic book noise | 89% | **99%** | ~93% |
| Heavy adversarial noise | 57% | **88%** | ~72% |
| One real page, merged text | 65% precision | **98%** | — |

False certainty — a move marked certain that is wrong — is 0 on 7 of 8 runs.
That is the number that matters. A reader that says "certain" and is wrong is
worse than one that says "unsure", because nobody checks it.

Figurine notation, a real printed move list of glyphs with move numbers
interleaved: **16/16**, 12 proved, all 6 move numbers set aside.

## The part worth remembering

The first version passed a hand-made 20-move test **20/20** and was nearly
worthless. Building an evaluation harness and running 8,000 real tokens through
it scored it at **55.3% -> 58.1%** and exposed six bugs, including a square-repair
pass that never executed and a consensus stage voting on words from different
parts of the page.

Every number in the docs came from that harness. None came from reading the code.

## Environment

Engines in `/opt/chessguru-vision/.venv-ocr`, deliberately separate from the live
scanner's venv — Surya, Paddle and docTR each pin their own torch/numpy and a bad
resolve would take board scanning offline. Live vision service confirmed healthy
throughout.

Surya pinned to 0.16.7, NOT latest: 0.22 replaced its PyTorch backend with a
`llama-server` binary from llama.cpp. Needs `transformers < 5`; nothing else in
that venv uses transformers.
